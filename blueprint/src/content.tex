\chapter{The framework}

The five results the case study rests on, and the constructions they are proved for.

\section{Systems, executions, and trace distributions}

A PLTS is the single model every system of this development is written in.
Executions record whole runs and traces record what is observable of them, and that separation is what lets an implementation be compared with its specification.
A refinement claim here is always an inclusion $\operatorname{ATD}(sys_C) \subseteq \operatorname{ATD}(sys_A)$ of achievable trace distributions, and safety properties are read off the supports of those distributions.
Throughout, $\tau$ is the distinguished silent label of the label type, a label is internal when it equals $\tau$ and external otherwise, $\delta_s$ is the Dirac distribution on $s$, $\mu \triangleright g$ is the pushforward of $\mu$ along $g$, and $s[x := v]$ is record update.
See \leanmodule{Leslie2/Systems/System} and \leanmodule{Leslie2/Systems/Trace}.

\section{Weak transitions and the two constructions}

Weak transitions absorb internal computation, and all three of them move a distribution over states rather than a state and run until an internal scheduler halts.
The two constructions closing the section turn those notions into ordinary systems --- the weak closure $\cdot^w$ makes a weak transition a single step, and the distribution-monad lift $\mathcal{D}(\cdot)$ makes a distribution over states a single state --- and the soundness proof of Section~\ref{sec:soundness} factors through their composite.

\section{Probabilistic simulations}

A simulation of a concrete system by an abstract one is carried by a relation together with a step-matching condition, phrased through a coupling of the two successor distributions.
The two notions of this section differ only in what the abstract side may answer a concrete step with: a single step for strong simulation, and a weak transition out of a belief distribution over abstract states for forward simulation.
Every refinement of the case study is proved in the second of them, whose belief distribution is where an abstract commitment is deferred to.

\chapter{The ABA case study}

This section verifies an implementation of Asynchronous Byzantine Agreement against a small specification, by probabilistic forward simulation, on the five results proved above.
The setting is $n$ processes, each holding an input bit, of which at most $f$ are Byzantine, with $3f < n$, communicating by asynchronous message passing.
The development lives in the opt-in Lake library \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols}.

\section{Setting and method}

Three protocols occur in the case study, each given as properties over the calls and returns of its interface that speak only of processes never corrupted.
What this development establishes is Validity and Agreement for ABA, machine-checked; Termination, Unpredictability and fairness are out of scope, and the weak common coin is held at specification level, so $\varepsilon$-Goodness enters as an assumption.

Asynchronous Byzantine Agreement (ABA) takes a bit from each process and may return a bit.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Validity: a correct process returns only a bit a correct process had as input.
  \item Agreement: two correct returns carry the same bit.
  \item $\varepsilon$-sure Termination: once $n-f$ correct processes take part, all correct processes return with probability $1-\varepsilon$.
\end{itemize}

Graded Binding Crusader Agreement (GBCA) takes a bit and may return a graded value in $\{(0,A),\ (0,B),\ (\bot,C),\ (1,B),\ (1,A)\}$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Validity: a correct process failing to return $(b,A)$ implies a correct process had $1-b$ as input.
  \item Graded Agreement: returns $(b,X)$ and $(b',X')$ of correct processes satisfy $b = 0 \Rightarrow b' \neq 1$ and $X = A \Rightarrow X' \neq \bot$.
  \item Binding: when a correct process returns, a bound value $b$ is defined and no extension of the execution returns $(1-b,B)$ or $(1-b,A)$ to a correct process.
  \item Termination: once $n-f$ correct processes take part, all correct processes return.
\end{itemize}

Weak Common Coin (WCC) takes no input and may return a bit.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\varepsilon$-Goodness: with probability $\varepsilon$ all correct processes return $0$, and with probability $\varepsilon$ all return $1$.
  \item Unpredictability: all outcomes remain possible until $f+1$ processes have called.
  \item $(1-\failmass)$-sure Termination: once $n-f$ correct processes take part, all correct processes return with probability $1-\failmass$.
\end{itemize}

Every protocol with sub-protocols is treated in two moves: substitution, in which each sub-implementation is exchanged for the corresponding specification in a fixed context and which is sound by precongruence, and the core simulation, in which the core algorithm beside the specifications of its sub-protocols is simulated by the protocol's own specification.
Composing the two inclusions places the implementation inside the specification, and the recursion stops at WCC, which is left at specification level.
GBCA is verified against two implementations: a direct one, which calls no sub-protocol, and a gather-based one, through which the recursion continues into gather and reliable broadcast.

\paragraph{Object names.}
Transition System~1 is the ABA specification, Transition System~2 the GBCA specification, Transition System~3 the WCC specification, Transition System~4 the gather specification and Transition System~6 the BRB specification; the numbering is the source's, whose Transition Systems~5 and~7 (SRSD and AVSS) are not encoded, and the names are used throughout this section and in every node of it.
A rule is cited by its Lean constructor name, a deviation label in parentheses marks a point where an encoding departs from~\cite{LeslieBP}, and where a node names ABDY22's Algorithm~$k$ it means the numbering of~\cite{ABDY22}.
Bracha's third message level is $\mathrm{VOTE}$ here and in the source blueprint, where both~\cite{ABDY22} and~\cite{AFW25} write $\mathrm{READY}$.
Its echo threshold is $n-f$ here and in the source blueprint, where~\cite{AFW25} writes $(n+f)/2$.
The encoding follows the source blueprint on both points, so neither carries a deviation label.

\paragraph{Outside the model.}
Two features of~\cite{LeslieBP} are deliberately not encoded: fairness, whose markings bear only on liveness, and partial information, whose observation functions and belief construction are not needed because every adversary here is omniscient.

\paragraph{Notation.}
Write $[n]$ for the identifier set and $F \subseteq [n]$ for the corrupted set.

\section{Deviations from the source blueprint}
\label{sec:aba-deviations}

Each departure from~\cite{LeslieBP} carries a label, cited at the point where it applies; the labels D2, D3, D6, D7 and D20 are not in use.
\begin{itemize}
  \item D1: corruption is a guarded deterministic transform, broadcast to every component.
    The label $\mathrm{fail}(id)$ fires only under $id \notin F \wedge |F| < f$, the guard sitting on the row of the component that owns the corrupted set, so a corruption fires exactly where it takes effect.
    The transform is $\mathrm{corrupt}$, which inserts $id$ into $F$ and writes nothing else.
    The half of the broadcast taken at the process the label names replaces that process's program, the replacement itself being D23.
  \item D4: the WCC \texttt{guess} label and the \texttt{guess} state field are omitted.
  \item D5: a set-based authenticated network with per-sender message pools.
  \item D8: protocol sends gated on prior participation.
  \item D9: $0$-based round indexing.
  \item D10: the per-round bookkeeping between the coin return and the next call fused into the $retW$ step, which multicasts DECIDED on an $A$ grade.
  \item D11: Byzantine call- and return-driver rules at each handshake.
    A replaced program's sub-protocol traffic is the drives' business; its interface traffic is D23's.
  \item D12$'$: per-process DECIDED sent and received pools, delivery per (receiver, sender, bit), and Byzantine equivocation in those pools.
  \item D13: the Validity provenance apparatus on Transition System~1, namely a ghost record $input$ of the genuine $\mathrm{callABA}$ events and an $f+1$ support guard \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.SuppOK} on the decision.
    The record has two writers, the interface rules \leandeclnamed{callSet}{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep.callSet} and \leandeclnamed{callLoop}{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep.callLoop}, and the support guard on the decision is what turns it into provenance.
  \item D14: $f+1$ support guards on the GBCA specification's binding, on the dissenting bit at its $B$-return, and on each of the two bits at its $C$-return.
  \item D15: the $F$-blind form of those support counts, namely $f+1$ distinct callers-or-corrupted.
  \item D16: the two-phase abstract twin used by the core simulation, which defers the specification's $\tau$-progress to a single \leandeclnamed{decide}{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep.decide} step under the first visible return.
    The overwrite performed by \leandeclnamed{callSet}{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep.callSet} is what makes the deferral sound: a bit written to the record by the unguarded loop rule stays revisable until a value is decided.
  \item D17: a fourth outcome $\dagger$, of mass $\failmass$, on the single distribution both coin resolutions follow.
    At Transition System~3 it is a resolution that delivers to no process.
    At Transition System~1 it is the kill outcome of the flip, which drives the system into the terminal mode $dead$, and $dead$ enables no $\tau$-rule.
  \item D18: the GBCA implementation transcribes ABDY22's Algorithm~6 in full, six rounds over the message levels INPUT, ECHO, VOTE, BIND and SEAL, in place of the source's four-round compression of it, which elides the SEAL level and takes $f{+}1$ $\mathrm{VOTE}\,v$ receipts as the grade-$1$ witness and thereby violates Binding.
  \item D19: the GBCA specification state carries an exclusion set $dead \subseteq \{0,1\}$, the bits the instance is barred from handing out, in place of the source's bound value $bind \in \{0,1,\bot\}$, which makes Binding negative and monotone.
  \item D21: Transition System~1 is eight rules over the record $\{input, ret, F, val, mode\}$, in place of the source's per-process call row and five-valued coin.
    The coin is an abstract flip, \leandeclnamed{coinFlip}{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep.coinFlip}, which resolves once per firing into a lock, a release or a kill and names no bit.
    Its guards are $mode = idle$, $val = \bot$ and the mixedness gate, which asks $f+1$ support of each of the two bits.
    The decision is a single guarded write, \leandeclnamed{decide}{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep.decide}, of a bit carrying $f+1$ $F$-blind support.
  \item D22: retention with termination.
    A process record retains the graded-agreement stage record of every round it has touched, in a finite map \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.Net.StageSideRec} on which a round never touched reads as the initial stage record.
    The stage-side rules are round-indexed and guarded by the stage record their label tags rather than by the round loop, so a process answers the traffic of a round its round loop has passed.
    That indefinite participation is the regime of ABDY22's Lemmas~4.6 and~E.5, which are stated under the hypothesis that no non-faulty process has terminated.
    Termination is the rule \leandeclnamed{terminate}{PLTS.ABA.Net.FlatProcStep.terminate}, which fires once the process's own return has fired and $2f+1$ DECIDED receipts are on its record (ABDY22's Definitions~3.1 and~3.2 and its note on termination), and whose effect is to set the flag alone.
    It stops the stage-side rules only: the DECIDED rules carry no termination guard, so a process that has terminated keeps relaying the payloads it holds.
    The rule carries the replacement guard of D23, so a process whose program has been replaced never terminates.
  \item D23: when a process is corrupted its program is replaced.
    The round-loop record carries the flag \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.CoreRec.corrupted}, written by the process's own half of the corruption broadcast, \leandeclnamed{failSelf}{PLTS.ABA.Net.FlatProcStep.failSelf}, and the record beneath the flag stands still from that point.
    Every honest row is enabled only at an unreplaced program, and in place of those rows the replaced program is the single self-loop \leandeclnamed{corruptedIdle}{PLTS.ABA.Net.FlatProcStep.corruptedIdle}, taken on every label other than $\tau$ and the labels on which the process would act on its own sub-protocol traffic, where it has no row at all.
    That traffic runs through the drives of D11.
    The two networks carry the Byzantine visible return, $\mathrm{retABA}(id,b)$ at a corrupted process for either bit under the authorisation $id \in F$ alone.
    The specification's corrupted interface rules are the image of the replacement: \leandeclnamed{callByz}{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep.callByz} records a bit unrelated to the one its label declares, and \leandeclnamed{retByz}{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep.retByz} returns any bit at any time without moving the state.
    The honest rules stay available to a corrupted process, so the two rules add behaviour and remove none.
  \item D24: the gather-based GBCA implementation follows Algorithm~4 of~\cite{AFW25} at $R = 1$, with the approximate-agreement stage omitted and the grade read off the second gather's counts directly --- $(v, A)$ at $|{\rm dom}\, h| - f$ entries of $v$, $(v, B)$ at $f{+}1$, $(\bot, C)$ below both.
    The source supplies no gather-based GBCA; its gather serves the coin construction, which stays at specification level here, so \cite{AFW25} is the algorithm's source of record and Transition System~2 is unchanged as its specification.
  \item D25: the gather specification carries a write-once \emph{core family} in its state --- a nonempty set of committed-entry sets, any two of which (each with itself) share at least $n - f$ entries, every return dominating some member --- in place of Transition System~4's single bound identifier set carried on its return labels.
    The classical common core is the family's intersection; its $n - f$ size bound is a fact about complete executions, not a state invariant an implementation can maintain (\cite{AFW25}, Remark~1), and the family is what a reachable state does determine.
    On the implementation side the $\mathrm{BIND}$ payload is sent by reliable broadcast rather than on the fabric, which is what pins a committed payload against a sender corrupted later.
  \item D26: the gather specification records calls and committed entries in separate fields, a per-entry internal commit writing $val(k)$ once under $k \in F \vee call(k) = v$, in place of Transition System~4's rule writing $call$ itself at a corrupted process.
    A process corrupted after its call may still direct its committed entry, and the guard reads $F$ at the commit rather than at the call.
  \item D27: the BRB specification splits the leader's recorded input from the committed value, an internal commit writing the value once under $\mathit{ldr} \in F \vee input = m$, in place of Transition System~6's single call field and its corrupted-leader rule, which only bars the instance from returning.
    A leader corrupted after its call may commit any value; Totality, a liveness property, is not encoded.
  \item D28: composite instances are single rule tables over joint states rather than synchronised compositions.
    A sub-protocol's call and return are fused into the rows of the caller --- the gather call contributes to the input broadcast, the $\mathrm{BIND}$ send is the bind-broadcast call, the first gather's return is the second's call --- and a sub-instance's delivery is a derived predicate on its coordinate, read by the consuming row, not an event of the composite.
\end{itemize}

\section{The specifications and their safety}

Everything in this section is what both implementations are measured against.
A specification locates the adversary differently from the implementation it stands for.
An implementation pairs processes that read only their own replacement flag with an explicit network-adversary PLTS, which owns the corrupted set together with its budget and authorises every Byzantine action by the guard $k \in F$ of a component; a specification carries no network component at all, and its adversarial transitions are bare nondeterministic choices resolved by the scheduler.
Leaving those choices unstructured is sound because a specification constrains from above: an unstructured adversary makes the specification more permissive, never less, and trace-distribution inclusion asks only that every behaviour of the implementation be matched by some resolution of the specification's nondeterminism.

\subsection{The ABA specification and its safety}

Transition System~1 is eight rules over the record \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.SpecState}, and its flip rule is the only probabilistic transition of the system.
Safety is proved by induction over those eight rules under two invariants, one on the state and one reading the label history.
The decision value $val$ is written once, by one rule guarded on $val = \bot$, and the $f+1$ $F$-blind support of the bit it writes is that same rule's guard, so provenance needs no separate invariant conjunct.
The budget pigeonhole then leaves one supporter never corrupted and ghost-recorded as having called the value, which is the caller Validity names.
Both trace predicates quantify over never-corrupted returners: a corrupted process returns an arbitrary bit at an arbitrary time (D23), which no property of the system constrains.

\section{The protocol reading}
\label{sec:aba-protocol-reading}

What runs is $n$ programs, a network adversary holding the message pools and the corrupted set with its budget, and a coin oracle held at specification level.
A program reads its own replacement flag and nothing else about corruption: not the corrupted set, not the budget, not another process's status (D23).
The rendezvous the shared alphabet cannot name are carried by an extended alphabet, hidden to $\tau$ and then discharged by the restriction, so what leaves the outermost frame is the alphabet the ABA specification speaks.
That much is fixed by the round loop and the specification, and is independent of how graded agreement is implemented, so it is written once, parametrically in the implementation.
Section~\ref{sec:aba-abdy} instantiates it at ABDY22's implementation and Section~\ref{sec:aba-gather} at the gather-based one.
A system below the meeting point therefore belongs to one implementation or the other, and carries a superscript naming its source: $\abdy{\mathrm{protocol}}(P)$ and $\abdy{\mathrm{composed}}(P)$ after~\cite{ABDY22}, and $\afw{\mathrm{protocol}}(P)$ and $\afw{\mathrm{composed}}(P)$ after~\cite{AFW25}.
The systems the two share, $\mathrm{hybrid}(P)$ and $\mathrm{spec}(P)$, carry none.
The Lean development writes each superscript as a namespace.

The control record beneath the reading holds one process's round loop, and no field of it names another process or holds the corrupted set, so locality sits in the state shape and not in the guards alone.
See \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Vocabulary/RoundLoop}, \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Reading/Alphabet} and \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Reading/Flat}.

\section{The ABDY implementation}
\label{sec:aba-abdy}

The first of the two implementations of graded agreement, and the protocol built on it.
The section runs from the stage a process executes to the inclusion that carries the protocol into its composed reading; what it establishes about the round loop and the ABA-side state is shared with Section~\ref{sec:aba-gather}, and what it establishes about graded agreement is not.

\subsection{The graded-agreement stage}

The first implementation of graded agreement calls no sub-protocol: it is ABDY22's Algorithm~6 transcribed in full, over five message levels.
The depth of those levels is what the grade-$1$ evidence buys (D18): $f{+}1$ $\mathrm{BIND}\,v$ receipts exceed the corruption budget, so an honest sender stands behind every grade-$1$ output, and that sender's own wait condition is the $n{-}f$ $\mathrm{VOTE}\,v$ quorum the binding argument of~\cite{ABDY22} counts.
The stage record holds one process's graded-agreement stage, and the definition below reads the round as one per-round PLTS, which is the form Theorem~\ref{thm:gbca-inst-refines} is proved against.
Its state is the pair of the $n$ stage records and the round's own message fabric, so the network is a component of it and not a field, and its guards read that pair through four accessors.

\subsection{The composed reading}

A sub-protocol may be exchanged for its specification where it is a component of a composition, and the protocol does not present graded agreement as one.
This subsection reads the same protocol as a composition of components, the cut being decided by which component owns the round's message fabric.
The component boundary is where the chain passes from implementation to specification: $\abdy{\mathrm{protocol}}(P)$ is the system that runs, everything above it is specification, and the first link is accordingly an inclusion.
The composed reading holds one graded-agreement instance per round at every moment, and a round instance carries no termination flag, so it answers a send or a delivery at a process the protocol has terminated (D22).
Graded agreement is a parallel component of that reading, which is what the substitution congruence consumes.

\subsection{The round loop}

The round loop is the core algorithm of ABA, and it is what calls the other two.
Its external return is gated on an $n-f$ DECIDED quorum, and that count is what excludes disagreement: two returns of different bits would need two sender sets of size $n-f$, and any two such sets meet in an honest process.
Its rules are written against one process's own record in Definition~\ref{def:aba-procnet}, and the state below is what those rules move, read across the two components that hold it.
Both implementations run the same round loop over the same ABA-side state.
See \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Vocabulary/RoundLoop} and \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/ABDY/ABAState}.

\section{The hybrid and the ABDY chain}

Replacing the graded-agreement family by its specification leaves $\mathrm{hybrid}(P)$, which is where the two implementations meet.
Everything from that point up is proved once: the core simulation into the ABA specification, the safety transfer, and the witnesses that the system runs at all.
The chain closes here for ABDY22's implementation, and Section~\ref{sec:aba-gather} rejoins it.

\subsection{The substitution}

The graded-agreement family occupies the refinable slot of the composed system, so exchanging it for its specification is one family congruence followed by the precongruences of the pipeline, and no transitivity of simulations enters.
What the idealisation removes is the round's message fabric and every Byzantine power over it; the DECIDED pools, the corrupted set with the budget $|F| < f$ as the guard on the $\mathrm{fail}$ row, and the authorisation of every Byzantine drive are outside the exchanged component and survive unchanged.
The system that results, $\mathrm{hybrid}(P)$, is where the two implementations meet: both reach it, and every step above it is proved once and shared.

\subsection{From the hybrid to the ABA specification}

The refinement of $\mathrm{hybrid}(P)$ into the specification is one hand-built simulation, run in the hybrid's own coordinates.

\subsection{The main theorems}

Three steps separate the protocol from the ABA specification:
\[

  \;\overset{\text{(1)}}{\sqsubseteq}\; \abdy{\mathrm{composed}}(P)
  \;\overset{\text{(2)}}{\sqsubseteq}\; \mathrm{hybrid}(P)
  \;\overset{\text{(3)}}{\sqsubseteq}\; \mathrm{spec}(P),

where (1) is the composition inclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-protocol-composed}, along the Dirac lift of $\mathrm{ProtocolRel}(P)$; (2) the substitution of Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-substitution}; and (3) the refinement of Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-hybrid-spec}, whose proof is the hand-built core simulation, read in the coordinates of $\mathrm{hybrid}(P)$. Safety then transfers by Theorem~\ref{thm:trace-support}, with no hypothesis on the trace beyond positive probability, and both properties are stated on the returns of never-corrupted processes.

\subsection{Non-vacuity}

A refinement statement is compatible with a system that does nothing.
The chain is therefore accompanied by machine-checked witnesses that the hybrid of Definition~\ref{def:aba-hybrid}, the system Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-hybrid-spec} is proved of, executes and reaches a return at $n = 4$ and $f = 1$, on a connected run of positive probability that exercises the one probabilistic transition on the way, and that the $\mathrm{fail}$ label is live in it rather than merely declared.
Membership of a decision trace in the achievable trace distributions of the protocol is not established; the witnesses are about that system alone.
Both chains pass through that system, so the witnesses serve both.

\section{The gather-based implementation}
\label{sec:aba-gather}

Transition System~2 has a second verified implementation.
Attiya, Flam and Welch~\cite{AFW25} build graded agreement from two calls to a \emph{gather} sub-protocol and local counting, and the source blueprint carries the matching material: a gather specification (Transition System~4), a gather implementation over reliable broadcast (its Algorithm~4, from~\cite{AFW25}), and Bracha's reliable broadcast (Transition System~6 and its Algorithm~6, from~\cite{Bra87}).
This section carries that construction to implementation level in full --- the two gather instances and, beneath them, the $4n$ Bracha instances --- and to the same specification: the round interface $\mathrm{callG}/\mathrm{retG}$, Transition System~2, and every statement downstream of it are those of the sections above, so the two implementations differ only in the system at the bottom of the chain.
The per-round work is three substitutions in sequence, each replacing one layer of sub-protocol by its specification; at the protocol shape the three stages land on $\mathrm{hybrid}(P)$, and the chain continues along the shared links.
The Lean files are \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Vocabulary/Fabric} through \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/AFW/Chain}, mapped file by file in the ABA library's own guide.

\subsection{Reliable broadcast}

Reliable broadcast is the bottom layer, and its specification is the first point dynamic corruption shapes: a leader corrupted after its call may still direct the delivered value, so the committed value is a separate field written by a guarded internal rule, not the call's own record (D27).
The implementation is Bracha's three message levels over the shared two-box state, and its refinement certifies the committed value by an $\mathrm{ECHO}$ receipt quorum, firing the abstract commit on demand under the first return.

\subsection{Gather}

Gather has every process broadcast a payload and returns to each caller a set of (process, payload) entries, with a binding guarantee: the returns share a committed core fixed no later than the first of them.
The specification holds the binding content as a write-once \emph{family} of entry sets rather than as one bound set (D25).
The single core of the classical statement is the family's intersection, and its $n - f$ size bound is a fact about complete executions --- at $n = 3f + 1$, a reachable state at the first return has as few as $n - 2f$ honest votes cast, and no state predicate pins one $n{-}f$-sized set every future return must dominate; what a state does determine is the family of committed $\mathrm{BIND}$ payloads, pairwise overlapping in $n - f$ entries.
Every counting argument the two-gather round needs is re-derived from that pairwise bound.
The implementation sends its $\mathrm{BIND}$ payloads by reliable broadcast, which is what pins a payload against a sender corrupted later, and is verified in two substitutions: the Bracha layer replaced coordinatewise, then the gather rounds replaced by Transition System~4.
See \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Gather/Spec}, \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Gather/Ideal}, \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Gather/IdealSim}, \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Gather/Low} and \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/Gather/LowSim}.

\subsection{The two-gather round}

The round calls gather on the input bits, takes as candidate the bit carried by all but $f$ of the returned entries, calls gather again on the candidates, and reads the grade off the counts of the second return (D24).
The refinement into Transition System~2 is pure counting on the two core families: a bit every member carries on fewer than $|U| - f$ entries is excluded and stays excluded, a member heavy at one candidate excludes the other, and the $A$/$C$ latch is member heaviness against member lightness.
The kill-on-demand discipline of the direct implementation's refinement carries over unchanged.

\section{The gather-based chain}

Beneath the composed reading is the protocol that runs.
It is Definition~\ref{def:aba-flat-reading} a second time, at the gather-based implementation: the same round loop, the same DECIDED pools, the same coin handshake, the same corruption discipline and the same pipeline, over a stage side that is the two gather instances and the $4n$ Bracha instances beneath them.
Two rearrangements separate that stage side from the composed reading's.
A round of the composed reading carries $4n+2$ message fabrics; the adversary of a flat reading carries one pool family, so a stage message is tagged with the fabric it belongs to.
And the composed reading indexes the boxes by instance and then by process, where a program must hold its own data and no one else's, so the stage record is transposed to hold one process's box in every instance.
Assembling the composed state from the flat one undoes both, which is what the simulation relation of Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-afw-protocol-composed} does.
See \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/AFW/Flat} and \leanmodule{Leslie2Protocols/ABA/AFW/FlatSim}.
