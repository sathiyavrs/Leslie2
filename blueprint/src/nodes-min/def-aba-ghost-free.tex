\begin{definition}[The ghost-free system]
  \label{def:aba-ghost-free}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.Implementation.systemGhostFree}\lean{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.ghostFreeProtocol}\lean{PLTS.ABA.AFW.ghostFreeProtocol}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-implementation, def:aba-protocol, def:aba-afw-protocol}
  The implementation of Definition~\ref{def:aba-implementation} at a one-element ghost: the same programs, the same network transitions and the same common coin, with the network holding nothing of a round and announcing no bound bit: \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.Implementation.systemGhostFree}.
  The ghost-free systems of the two protocols are that definition at the two implementations, \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.ghostFreeProtocol} and \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.AFW.ghostFreeProtocol}, each the protocol of Definition~\ref{def:aba-protocol} and of Definition~\ref{def:aba-afw-protocol} with the ghost dropped and nothing else changed.
  The read of the ghost enables the announcement and no other transition, which is what puts the three readings in one parametric system: each algorithm reads the bit its ghost holds, and the ghost-free system reads none (D30).
\end{definition}
