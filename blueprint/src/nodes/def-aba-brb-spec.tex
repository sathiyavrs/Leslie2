\begin{definition}[The BRB specification instance]
  \label{def:aba-brb-spec}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.BRB.Lab}\lean{PLTS.ABA.BRB.SpecState}\lean{PLTS.ABA.BRB.SpecState.corrupt}\lean{PLTS.ABA.BRB.Step}\lean{PLTS.ABA.BRB.specInst}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-params}
  Transition System~6, read safety-only over an instance-local alphabet.
  An instance is parametric in the payload type $M$ and names a leader; its alphabet is $\{\mathrm{call}(m), \mathrm{ret}(id, m), \mathrm{fail}(id), \tau\}$ and its state is the record $\{input, val, ret, F\}$, the leader's recorded input and the committed value held in separate fields (D27).
  Five rules: the call writes $input$ once, with its input-enabledness loop; the internal commit writes $val$ once, under the guard
  \[
    val = \bot \;\wedge\; \bigl(\mathit{ldr} \in F \vee input = m\bigr),
  \]
  so a corrupted leader commits any value and an honest one commits its input; the return hands out $val$ at a process that has not returned; and $\mathrm{fail}$ is the total corruption transform (D1).
  The split of $input$ from $val$ is what a leader corrupted after its call requires: until the commit fires, such a leader may still direct the committed value, and the guard reads $F$ at the commit rather than at the call.
  Agreement and Validity are structural --- $val$ has one writer and keeps its value, and at a never-corrupted leader the guard pins $val$ to $input$.
  Totality, a liveness property, is not encoded.
  Full construction: \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.BRB.specInst}.
\end{definition}
