\begin{definition}[The two-gather round]
  \label{def:aba-gbca-pair}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.cand}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.gradeOf}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.boundOfCore}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.ByAFW.PairState}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.ByAFW.PairStep}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.ByAFW.pairInst}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.ByAFW.pairInst_step_iff_row}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-round-sub, def:aba-gbca-net, def:aba-gather-spec, def:aba-gbca-spec, def:aba-labels}
  The graded agreement of~\cite{AFW25} --- its Algorithm~4 at $R = 2$, the two-gather branch, with the grade read off the second gather's counts in place of the approximate-agreement subroutine of lines~7 and~8 (D24) --- over two gather specification instances of Definition~\ref{def:aba-gather-spec}, each the binding form (D33), the first at payload type $\mathbb{B}$ and the second at $\mathbb{B} \cup \{\bot\}$.
  The round is the composition of Definition~\ref{def:aba-round-sub}: its state is the $n$ program records beside the layer's network, in parallel with the two gather states, and a program's record holds the process's input, its candidate, whether it has called the second gather, its graded outcome and its return flag.
  For a first-gather output $g$, the candidate is
  \[
    \mathrm{cand}(g) \;=\;
      \begin{cases}
        v & \text{if } |{\rm dom}\, g| - f \le \#\{k : g(k) = v\},\\
        \bot & \text{if neither bit qualifies,}
      \end{cases}
  \]
  at most one bit qualifying since the return domains carry $n - f > 2f$ entries; for a second-gather output $h$, the grade is $(v, A)$ at $|{\rm dom}\, h| - f$ entries of $v$, $(v, B)$ at $f + 1$, and $(\bot, C)$ below both.
  The rows are the round's transitions at the specification's labels, one case each: the round's $\mathrm{callG}$ is the caller's record of its input beside the first gather's call, and the family's call loops reach the first gather's call with the caller standing still; each gather's internal rules are silent rows; the first gather's return to a process, which records the candidate and writes the bound bit, and that process's call of the second gather at the candidate are two silent rows; the second gather's return, which records the grade, and the round's $\mathrm{retG}$, which announces it, are two rows likewise; and corruption is the lockstep transform of both gathers (D1).
  A gather specification answers its call label on two rows, so each of the three rows reaching a gather call carries the gather's step as a hypothesis, whichever row it takes.
  The layer's network holds the bound bit, auxiliary state (D29) that no program reads and no guard consults.
  It is written at the round's first $\mathrm{ret1}$ and once only, as $\mathrm{boundOfCore}$ of the core the first gather's return carries, and every graded return announces it on its label.
  For a payload set $S$, $\mathrm{boundOfCore}(S)$ is the bit heavy in $S$ --- carried by at least $|S| - f$ of its entries --- and $1$ where neither bit is.
  At the sizes the core rule of Definition~\ref{def:aba-gather-spec} allows, $|S| \ge n - f > 2f$, at most one bit is heavy, so a return handing out $v$ announces $v$ and a return handing out nothing announces a bit whose complement no return can hand out.
  Full construction: \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.ByAFW.pairInst}; the rule table is \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.ByAFW.PairStep}, and \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.ByAFW.pairInst_step_iff_row} states that the round's transitions over the family labels the pullback sends to one specification label are exactly its rows at that label.
\end{definition}
