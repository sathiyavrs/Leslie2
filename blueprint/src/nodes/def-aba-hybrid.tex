\begin{definition}[The hybrid]
  \label{def:aba-hybrid}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.HybridState}\lean{PLTS.ABA.hybridExtended}\lean{PLTS.ABA.hybrid}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-composed, def:aba-gbca-instance-by-abdy, def:aba-gbca-spec, def:idle-family, def:relabel, def:process-algebra}
  The composed system of Definition~\ref{def:aba-composed} with its graded-agreement component replaced by the family $\mathrm{gbcaSpecificationFamily}(P)$ of round specifications, the other three components and the whole pipeline unchanged:
  \[
    \mathrm{hybridExtended}(P) \;=\;
      \mathrm{gbcaSpecificationFamily}(P) \parallel \bigl(\mathrm{synchronisedProduct}_{j}\ \mathrm{roundLoopProgram}(P,j)
        \parallel (\mathrm{ABANetwork}(P) \parallel \mathrm{coinOverRoundAlphabet}(P))\bigr),
  \]
  and $\mathrm{hybrid}(P, M)$ is that read through the two frames of $\abdy{\mathrm{composed}}(P)$.
  Every component here is written over $\mathrm{ExtendedLabel}(n, M)$ and no step of the system reads a round message, so the hybrid is a system for every round message type $M$: the ABDY chain takes $\mathrm{GBCA.ByABDY.Message}$ for it, and the gather-based chain takes the empty type, so the round multicast and the round delivery name no label there. The system stays in the protocol's own coordinates: the round loops are $n$ programs, the DECIDED sets and the corrupted set are the ABA network's, and only the graded-agreement rounds are read at specification level.
  Full construction: \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.hybrid}.
\end{definition}
