\begin{definition}[The program of one process]
  \label{def:aba-programs}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.ByABDY.RoundVariables}\lean{PLTS.ABA.RoundLoopVariables}\lean{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.RoundVariablesMap}\lean{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.ProcessVariables}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Composition.NetworkEvent}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Composition.ExtendedLabel}\lean{PLTS.ABA.Implementation.networkEventLabels}\lean{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.RoundStep}\lean{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.ABAProgramStep}\lean{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.ABAProgram}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-labels, def:aba-round-messages, def:aba-round-loop-state, def:aba-implementation, def:relabel, def:synchronised-product}
  Process $j$ runs Definition~\ref{def:aba-implementation} at ABDY22's implementation of Definition~\ref{def:aba-round-messages}: the round message type is $\mathrm{GBCA.ByABDY.Message}$, the round variables are $\mathrm{GBCA.ByABDY.RoundVariables}(n)$, and the round's transitions are the sixteen of \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.RoundStep} --- the graded-agreement call and its loop at a round already called, the nine multicasts of those levels, the delivery that files a received message and its loop at a terminated program, and the three returns.
  The twenty-nine transitions around them are the shared ones.
  The state of process $j$ is
  \[
    \mathrm{ProcessVariables}(n) \;=\;
      \mathrm{RoundLoopVariables}(n) \times \mathrm{RoundVariablesMap}(n),
  \]
  its round-loop variables --- the control state of Definition~\ref{def:aba-round-loop-state}, the DECIDED received sets $decidedDelivered(k)$ and the record $decidedSent$ of the DECIDED bits $j$ has multicast --- beside its round variables.
  The second factor holds a finite map $roundVariablesMap$ from rounds to the round variables of Definition~\ref{def:aba-round-messages}, each with its received sets $received(k)$, and a flag $terminated$ (D22).
  The variables of round $r$ are read $\mathrm{roundVariables}\,r$: the retained ones where $j$ has touched round $r$, the initial round variables elsewhere.
  The map is finite by its type, so $j$ holds the variables of finitely many rounds at every point of a run, and the round advance resets none of them.
  Every round's transition is accordingly round-indexed rather than round-guarded: it reads and writes the variables of the round its own label tags, whichever round the round loop is in.
  Process $j$ therefore takes the transitions of a round its round loop has passed, and a round delivery of any round is filed.
  The transition $terminate$ fires on $j$'s own return and $2f+1$ received DECIDED messages, and its effect is to set $terminated$ and nothing else.
  Every round transition but the two loops carries the guard $terminated = \mathrm{false}$, so the retained round variables remain unchanged from that point, and a round delivery to a terminated program is taken on $\mathrm{deliverLoop}$.
  The call at a round not yet called carries the termination guard and its loop $\mathrm{callGLoop}$ asks for an input, so a process that has terminated at phase $toCallG$ over round variables holding no input has no transition on its call; the call is the program's output, so the refusal is the program's own.
  The DECIDED transitions carry no termination guard, so a process that has terminated keeps relaying the payloads it holds.
  The return $retABA$ and the DECIDED relay are guarded by the round-loop variables holding an input, so a process that was never called neither returns nor relays (D8); the relay asks for $b \notin decidedSent$ and the return for $b \in decidedSent$, the program's own record of its sends (D12$'$).
  There is no copy of the corrupted set or its budget, so every guard of the automaton $\mathrm{ABAProgram}(P,j)$ reads $j$'s own variables.
  A corruption replaces $j$'s program (D23).
  The round-loop variables carry the flag \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.RoundLoopVariables.corrupted}, written by $j$'s own half of the corruption broadcast, \leandeclnamed{failSelf}{PLTS.ABA.Implementation.ProgramStep.failSelf}; every other transition above is enabled only while that flag is not set, so the variables are unchanged from the corruption and $terminate$ in particular never fires afterwards.
  In place of those transitions the replaced program is the single self-loop \leandeclnamed{corruptedIdle}{PLTS.ABA.Implementation.ProgramStep.corruptedIdle}, taken on every non-silent label outside $\mathrm{actsAt}\,j$, the outputs of $j$ on which it takes no transition at all.
  The replaced program thus takes every input, and outputs its DECIDED relay and its visible return at either bit; the network takes the return on one unguarded transition.
  A corrupted process's graded-agreement call and coin call are outputs of the network (D11), and its round messages and DECIDED messages enter through the injections $\mathrm{byzantineGBCA}$ and $\mathrm{byzantineDecided}$.
  It runs over the extended alphabet $\mathrm{ExtendedLabel}(n, M) = \mathrm{Label}(n) \oplus \mathrm{NetworkEvent}(n, M)$, over the type $M$ of the messages a graded-agreement round exchanges, where $\mathrm{NetworkEvent}$ names the synchronisations the shared alphabet cannot: the round multicast $\mathrm{gbcaSend}(r,j,m)$ and the round delivery $\mathrm{gbcaDeliver}(r,i,j,m)$; the DECIDED send $\mathrm{decidedSend}(j,b)$ of a grade-2 round, the DECIDED relay $\mathrm{decidedRelay}(j,b)$ on $f+1$ received messages and the DECIDED delivery $\mathrm{decidedDeliver}(i,j,b)$; the round-internal call or return $\mathrm{gbcaRoundEvent}(r,j,e)$ at the boundary between the round's program and one of its sub-protocol instances, which sends no message; and a corrupted process's graded-agreement call $\mathrm{byzantineCallG}(r,k,b)$ and coin call $\mathrm{byzantineCallW}(r,k)$ (D11).
  The set $\mathrm{networkEventLabels}(n)$ of these labels is what the composition hides, the silent label of $\mathrm{ExtendedLabel}(n, M)$ being that of $\mathrm{Label}(n)$.
  Only the round multicast and the round delivery carry a message, so every component written over the alphabet is written for every $M$; the ABDY chain takes $\mathrm{GBCA.ByABDY.Message}$ for it and the gather-based chain of Section~\ref{sec:aba-gather} takes the empty type.
  Every transition is Dirac, and $terminate$ is the one transition of the list that fires on $\tau$.
  At an unreplaced program of $j$, every input of $j$ has a transition of process $j$ at every state, the participant's, a loop or an idle self-loop (Definition~\ref{def:aba-implementation}); a corrupted process's two calls are taken on idle self-loops at every program, the one the label names included.
  Full synchronisation therefore lets the program that outputs a label move while every other program stands.
  The full list of transitions, forty-five of them --- twenty-nine shared, sixteen of ABDY22's: \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.ABAProgram}.
\end{definition}
