\begin{theorem}[The gather-based composition]
  \label{thm:aba-afw-protocol-composed}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.AFW.roundProjection}\lean{PLTS.ABA.AFW.BoundInvariant}\lean{PLTS.ABA.AFW.ProtocolRelation}\lean{PLTS.ABA.AFW.protocolSimulation}\lean{PLTS.ABA.AFW.protocol_composed}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-afw-protocol, def:aba-afw-round-families, thm:forward-sound, def:achievable}
  The gather-based protocol is forward-simulated by its composed system, and hence every trace distribution it achieves is achieved there.
  The relation has eight conjuncts in the shape of Theorem~\ref{thm:aba-protocol-composed}: the round loops and the common coin are shared objects; the ABA network is the DECIDED sets beside the corrupted set and the started rounds; the rounds in the map of the composed family are the started rounds (D37); every round of the family is the projection $\mathrm{roundProjection}$ of the implementation's state; at a round outside the started rounds no process has sent, the ghost holds nothing and no process holds variables; every process of the corrupted set has a replaced program (D23); and \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.AFW.BoundInvariant} holds.
  The first five determine the composed state from the implementation's, up to the corruptions the family has recorded; the last three are invariants of the implementation's state.
  Assembling the projection undoes the two separations between the implementation and the composed system --- the local states are transposed back out of the round variables the $n$ processes hold, and each network's sent set is projected out of the network's single tagged sent-set family --- and it takes the two gathers' cores and the round's network state from the network's ghost for that round.
  $\mathrm{BoundInvariant}$ states what the projection needs of the ghost: at a process holding a round-$r$ candidate the first gather's core of round $r$ is recorded, at a process returned at round $r$ the round's bound bit is recorded, and the recorded bit is $\mathrm{boundOfCore}$ of the recorded first core.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-afw-protocol, def:aba-afw-round-families, def:forwardsim}
  Every transition writes the acting process's round variables and records at most one tagged message, and one lemma says what that does to the projection: the round the transition names reads as the one-point update of each of its coordinates.
  What a transition then owes is sent set algebra --- which of the six projected sent sets takes the message and which are left alone --- and the projection commutes with a sent set write in exactly those two ways.
  A graded-agreement call at an unreplaced program records the called round among the started rounds, and the family enters that round in its map on the same call; every other transition at round $r$ is guarded by the started rounds or reads variables of round $r$ other than the initial ones, a message sent at round $r$ or the ghost of round $r$, so $r$ is a round of the family's map, where the family takes the round's silent transitions.
  On that basis each transition of the implementation is matched by the composed round: a send and a delivery are hidden events of the round, matched by one silent step, the network's authenticity conjunct becoming membership in the projected sent set; the graded-agreement call is the round's own call or its loop and the second gather's call the round's event for it; a gather's return is that gather's return event, the first gather's return followed by the gather's announcement of its core, which the round's network records; the call of a process's own input broadcast is the call event of that instance inside the gather it belongs to; and a broadcast instance's return is the event that records what it returned.
  The graded return is the round's own return, and the network's announcement of the bound bit is the round's announcement on the same label: where the ghost of round $r$ holds $b$, the round's network state records a first core $C$ with $b = \mathrm{boundOfCore}(C)$ and records the round's return, which is the guard of the round's announcement.\lean{PLTS.ABA.AFW.BoundInvariant.announceBound_networkProjection}\leanok{}
  The remaining labels never reach a round.
  The ABA interface, the coin's call and return, the DECIDED send and the other DECIDED transitions move the round loop and the ABA network while the family of rounds is unchanged; corruption is one broadcast, the corrupted set the network holds being the corrupted set of every network state under the same guard.
  A corrupted process's graded-agreement call leaves the protocol's state where it stands and is matched by the empty run, and its coin call by the composed system's own, which the ABA network outputs under $k \in F$ at a started round (D11, D37); the replaced program's return is the replaced round loop's self-loop (D23), and a round delivery at a replaced or terminated program and the termination of a process are matched by the empty run.
  A program's transition on a label of $\mathrm{roundOwn}(j)$ is a transition of the implementation, which is what lets each of those cases rule the others out.
  The fields of the round no guard of a program reads --- the core of each of its two gather instances and the round's network state --- are computed from the network's ghost at the implementation, and a transition's ghost write is the round's write of them.
  The projection of the round the transition names is the one-point update of those coordinates, \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.AFW.roundProjection_writeGhost}; every other round's projection stands where it is, \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.AFW.roundProjection_writeGhost_ne}; and a transition whose write returns the ghost it found moves no round's projection at all, \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.AFW.roundProjection_ghostId}.
\end{proof}
