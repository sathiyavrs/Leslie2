\begin{theorem}[The gather-based composition]
  \label{thm:aba-afw-protocol-composed}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.AFW.toPair}\lean{PLTS.ABA.AFW.ProtocolRel}\lean{PLTS.ABA.AFW.protocolSim}\lean{PLTS.ABA.AFW.protocol_composed}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-afw-protocol, def:aba-afw-sides, thm:forward-sound, def:achievable}
  The gather-based protocol is forward-simulated by its composed reading, and hence every trace distribution it achieves is achieved there.
  The relation has five conjuncts: the round loops and the coin oracle are shared objects, the ABA-side network is the DECIDED sets beside the corrupted set, each round's instance is the view $\mathrm{toPair}$ of the flat state, and the bound bit of every round whose second gather has been called is on record.
  The first four are unguarded, so they determine the composed state from the flat one; the fifth, \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.AFW.BoundInv}, is the one clause that is not a reading of the flat state.
  Assembling the view undoes the two rearrangements the flat reading performs --- the local states are transposed back out of the round records the $n$ processes hold, and each network state is sliced out of the adversary's single tagged sent-set family --- and it reads the two gathers' cores and the round's bound bit off the adversary's ghost record of that round.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-afw-protocol, def:aba-afw-sides, def:forwardsim}
  Every row writes the acting process's round record and records at most one tagged message, and one lemma says what that does to the view: the round the row names reads as the one-point update of each of its coordinates.
  What a row then owes is sent set algebra --- which of the six slices takes the message and which are left alone --- and slicing commutes with a sent set write in exactly those two ways.
  On that basis each row of the implementation is answered by a row of the round instance: a send and a delivery are internal to the instance, so the composed reading answers them with one of its own silent rules, the adversary's authenticity conjunct becoming membership in the sliced sent set; the graded-agreement call and return are the instance's own call and return.
  The remaining labels never reach the implementation.
  The ABA interface, the coin handshake, the DECIDED rows and the fused coin return move the round loop and the ABA-side network while the family of rounds stands still; corruption is one broadcast, the corrupted set the adversary holds being the corrupted set of every network state under the same guard; and the two Byzantine graded-agreement rows have no row at the process they name.
  A program's row on a label of $\mathrm{stageOwn}(j)$ is a row of the implementation, which is what lets each of those cases rule the others out.
  The three fields of the round instance that no guard of it reads --- the core of each of its two gather instances and the round's bound bit --- are the adversary's ghost record on the flat side, and a row's ghost write is the instance's write of them.
  The view of the round the row names is the one-point update of those three coordinates, \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.AFW.toPair_writeGhost}; every other round's view stands where it is, \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.AFW.toPair_writeGhost_ne}; and a row whose write returns the record it found moves no round's view at all, \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.AFW.toPair_ghostId}.
  The link is the row that writes the first gather's core and the bound bit, and it is the only row that extends the fifth conjunct, so the bit a graded return announces is on record when the return fires.
\end{proof}
