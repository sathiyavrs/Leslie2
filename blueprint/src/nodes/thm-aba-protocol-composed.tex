\begin{theorem}[The protocol under its composed system]
  \label{thm:aba-protocol-composed}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.ProtocolState}\lean{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.ProtocolRelation}\lean{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.protocolRelation_mk}\lean{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.protocolRelation_init}\lean{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.protocolSimulation}\lean{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.protocol_composed}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-composed, def:aba-programs, def:aba-network, def:aba-protocol, def:forwardsim, def:coupling, def:achievable, thm:forward-sound}
  For all parameters $P$, the protocol is forward-simulated by its composed system along the Dirac lift of the relation $\mathrm{ProtocolRelation}(P)$, and hence
  \[
    \operatorname{ATD}(\abdy{\mathrm{protocol}}(P)) \;\subseteq\;
    \operatorname{ATD}(\abdy{\mathrm{composed}}(P)).
  \]
  The two presentations hold the same round data indexed two ways, by process at the protocol and by round at the composed system.
  $\mathrm{ProtocolRelation}(P)$ fixes every coordinate of a composed state that a state of the protocol determines, in five unguarded conjuncts: the round loops agree, replacement flags included (D23); the common coin is the same component in both systems; the ABA network is the network's DECIDED sets beside its corrupted set; the network state of round $r$ is the network's round-$r$ sent sets beside the same corrupted set and its ghost for that round, the round's bound bit; and the entry of process $j$ in the instance of round $r$ is the variables of round $r$ that $j$ holds, a round $j$ has not touched reading as the initial round variables in both systems.
  A composed state is therefore determined by any state of the protocol related to it.
  The determination is not injective: no composed state carries a termination flag, so two states of the protocol differing only in which processes have terminated are related to the same composed state (D22).
  Headline: \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.protocol_composed}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-composed, def:aba-programs, def:aba-network, def:aba-protocol, def:forwardsim, def:coupling, def:achievable, thm:forward-sound}
  The initial states are related, \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.protocolRelation_init}: everything is initial, so each process's map has no entry at any round and $\mathrm{roundVariables}\,r$ is the initial round variables there, which is the entry the composed initial state carries.
  For the step condition, take a state of the protocol, a composed state related to it, and a transition out of that protocol state.
  Every transition of the protocol is a product of Dirac factors beside the common coin's successor distribution, and so is the composed transition that matches it.
  The two presentations carry the same coin, so the coupling is functional in the coin coordinate and the matched outcomes differ in no coordinate the relation constrains.
  The transitions are matched by label class, each by the composed transition carrying the same label, and three classes carry the work.
  A round's transition writes the variables of the round its label tags and no other, and the round instance of that round writes the entry of the acting process and no other, so the fifth conjunct is preserved entry by entry; the two graded-agreement return transitions read the round's ghost for the bit they announce and write it back, so the fourth conjunct is restored by $rel\_setBound$ with every other round's ghost unchanged; and the transitions that write a field no conjunct reads --- the round advance and $terminate$ --- are matched by a stutter.
  A corrupted process's replaced program is matched loop for loop (D23), and its visible return by the ABA network's Byzantine transition, which requires $id \in F$ and none of the DECIDED messages, as the protocol adversary's does.
  Two classes of composed transition have no protocol counterpart, which is what makes the inclusion one-directional: the Byzantine graded-agreement call and return name a process whose program has no transition for them (D11, D22) under a full synchronisation, and the round transitions of a round instance carry no termination guard, so the instance answers a send or a delivery at a process the protocol has terminated.
  Soundness (Theorem~\ref{thm:forward-sound}) turns the simulation \leandecl{PLTS.ABA.ABDY.protocolSimulation} into the stated inclusion.
\end{proof}
