\begin{theorem}[Safety of the ABA specification]
  \label{thm:aba-spec-safe}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.spec_safe}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-spec, thm:trace-support}
  Every trace in the support of every achievable trace distribution of the repaired specification satisfies Validity and Agreement.\lean{PLTS.ABA.ValidityTrace}\leanok\lean{PLTS.ABA.AgreementTrace}\leanok{} Both predicates are read at never-corrupted returners, which is the quantification of the papers' own contracts: a corrupted process returns an arbitrary bit at an arbitrary time (D23), which no property of the system constrains.
  Validity is the paper form: every return $\mathrm{retABA}\,id\,b$ at trace position $m$ by a never-corrupted $id$ is preceded by a call $\mathrm{callABA}\,id'\,b$ at some $k < m$, where $id'$ is never corrupted anywhere along the trace and that call is $id'$'s first $\mathrm{callABA}$ of the trace.
  A process has one input, and its first call is the event that carries it.
  Agreement is that any two returns by never-corrupted processes carry the same bit.
  Corruption is read at the trace level, as non-membership in every stage of the fold of the D1 transform over the $\mathrm{fail}$ labels seen so far.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-spec, thm:trace-support}
  By Theorem~\ref{thm:trace-support} it suffices to prove the two predicates on the events of every genuine execution, which we do by induction over the eight rules with the invariants $\mathrm{SpecInv}$ and $\mathrm{ValInv}$.
  For Agreement, the decision value $val$ is write-once by the shape of the system: \leandeclnamed{decide}{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep.decide} is its only writer and fires only from $val = \bot$.
  A never-corrupted returner lies outside the corruption fold at its own position, hence outside the pre-state's corrupted set, so \leandeclnamed{retByzantine}{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep.retByzantine} is unavailable to it and its return reads that one value.
  For Validity, $\mathrm{SpecInv}$ ties $val$ to an $f{+}1$ $F$-blind support, each member of which is either ghost-recorded as inputting the value or corrupted, and $\mathrm{ValInv}$'s clause $input\_src$ attributes each ghost record to the first $\mathrm{callABA}$ of its process in the label history.
  Its converse clause $src\_input$ carries that attribution through \leandeclnamed{callSet}{PLTS.ABA.SpecStep.callSet}: the rule's guard is the empty entry, which at an uncorrupted process says that no earlier $\mathrm{callABA}$ of that process stands in the history, so the label the rule carries is the first.
  The at most $f$ ever-corrupted processes, obtained as a trace-level fold of the $\mathrm{failSet}$, leave one of the $f{+}1$ supporters a never-corrupted recorded caller by a budget pigeonhole.
  Positionality of that caller, a call at some $k < m$, is read off the same label-history fold.
\end{proof}
