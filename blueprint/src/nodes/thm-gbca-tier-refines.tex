\begin{theorem}[The two substitutions inside the round]
  \label{thm:gbca-tier-refines}
  \lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.LowPairRel}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.lowPairRefines}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.IdealRel}\lean{PLTS.ABA.GBCA.idealRefines}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-gbca-gather-low, def:aba-gbca-pair, thm:gather-low-refines, thm:gather-core}
  Each reading of the round is forward-simulated by the one above it, along the componentwise pairing of the gather-level relation with the equality of the bound coordinate: the fully concrete instance by the instance over broadcast components, along Theorem~\ref{thm:gather-low-refines}'s relation at both gather coordinates, and that instance by the two-gather round over specifications, along Theorem~\ref{thm:gather-core}'s.
  The round's bound bit is the third factor of each of the three states, and neither substitution touches it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}\leanok
  \uses{def:aba-gbca-gather-low, def:aba-gbca-pair, thm:gather-low-refines, thm:gather-core, def:forwardsim}
  The gather simulations export their answers as $\tau$-chains of the component instance closed by the answering row --- the internal reach, the fused call, and the return run --- and each row of the pair carries one component's row as a premise.
  The proof maps the exported chain into the pair coordinatewise, the other coordinate standing still, and closes it with the pair's own row; the link row concatenates the first coordinate's return run and the second coordinate's call answer into one silent run.
  Corruption is the lockstep transform on both sides, answered by the componentwise broadcast-compatibility of the gather relations.
\end{proof}
